TAIS

A skinny girl with two braids, TAIS (18) storms down a dark, long and narrow corridor on a
worn magnet board. Her gray jacket with shortish sleeves flutters as she accelerates further from
the light, and her crude, dusty boots tilt back and forth as she weaves and turns into the dark,
narrow tunnels ahead. A group of children, all between 8 and 13 years of age, are in pursuit; eyes
locked upon Tais’ heavy, worn backpack.

There is noise and commotion; the chasers are evidently entertained by the chase. But the
darkness grows, the tunnels become more unknown, and the backpack they had been tracking
drifts further from sight. Not before long, the youngest and less brave of the chasers start to lose
enthusiasm, and they start to glance increasingly more often at their leader, and initiator of the
chase - EMMA (15), who eventually slows, disappointed. After a short talk they decide their
chase will not be fruitful tonight – it’s not clear which dangers and peril may lay in store in the
dark service sections of the space station.

Meanwhile, Tais, panting quietly; ears and eyes on full alert, has scurried behind a small
generator box, barely concealing her tattered boots, bare knees, and the rugged frame of her
board. Her adrenaline slowly subsides in the shadow, and what was once chaos, appears to turn
to calm. With cautious relief, she takes her trusty magnet board under her arm, and edges
forward to the place from which she earlier came - a small duct. She gently removes the grating,
holds her breath as she drops her backpack and magnet board before her, then shimmies her
body through, legs first, to the level below. She grips on to a pipe with one hand as she
awkwardly shifts the grating back into its former place, before dropping into further darkness.

“You’re a little late”. Felix (18), playfully laughs, as Tais regains her footing from her drop. His
light hair glistens a little from the dim lighting around, but his old, dusty clothing, like Tais’, is
as lifeless and worn as the grim piping, steel and panels which surround them. Felix hoists Tais’
backpack over his shoulder, and takes her board under his arm. Now presuming danger has
passed, they quickly navigate the dark corridors which they intimately know back to safety.
Along the way, Tais expresses her anger in failing her hunt; only managing to scavenge a few
packets of chips and chocolate bars from above. And all because of that red-haired idiot who
started shouting when Tais was about to seize her true goal; the buns from the automat.

“At least this girl earned herself several bonuses today!” Felix sarcastically quips. To which Tais
grudgingly replies: “they won’t help her much!”, knowing very they will have to save that idiot -
who obviously looks as if she is 15. “She’ll soon feel how it is to live in the cold and hunger as
we do!”. Sensing her bitterness and annoyance, Felix hugs and comforts Tais, reminding her that
they live to hunt another day.

Felix and Tais stop in front of a double metal door, adorned with embossed geometrical drawing;
a square sign consisting of two triangles – the sign of the Milky Way. Felix takes out a narrow
memory stick from his pocket and inserts it into the slot next to the edge of the door. Hissing
quietly, the halves of the door open, revealing yet another dark corridor. Felix and Tais enter,
and the door closes behind them.

Behind, Felix hugs the youngsters and tells them to go wash their hands. He talks to them

Tais shivers under a thin lukewarm stream of water. She quickly gets out of the small shower
unit. Her teeth chattering, she hurriedly puts on a clean T-shirt, shortish jersey, pants and socks.
Putting her arms into the sleeves of a clean hoodie already mended several times, she turns on
the shower and throws into the unit a gray jacket, gray pants, socks and a couple of T-shirts. She
rubs them with frozen fingers. She audibly mutters: “I hope you die, Eden. I hope you die!”

Felix, meanwhile, sits on a mattress next to the wall in a cramped room more or less arranged to
be a bedroom: a nightstand and a small bed. He calculates something on his tablet. In front of
him, there is a map of the level above, complete with heat sensors, stairs and ventilation shafts.
He presses a button, and the map transforms into 3D hologram. On the nightstand lies a cup of
tea, as well as a few pieces of toast and chocolate bars. Tais enters the room. She greedily starts
eating the food and drily thanks Felix for bringing everything here. Tais falls onto the bed,
drained and fatigued. She catches a glimpse of Felix, and realises he is working on the route to
be used for taking out the new ones. With eyes heavy, she grumbles: “new ones mean new
people who will eat.” Her eyes open wider and voice grows more awake: “Who is going to feed
and clothe them when we don’t even have enough for ourselves?” Felix, nonchalantly responds:
“if we don’t they’re gonna die.” Before he can even begin his second sentence, Tais closes her
eyes and is fast asleep. Felix keeps working.
